## Thinking in Systems, a System of Thoughts

> [!abstract]
> This blog explores how to effectively "think in systems" and align them with a shared purpose. In today’s interconnected world—where complexity is ever-increasing in software development, team dynamics, or organizational structures—the ability to model and understand interactions between components is crucial for success.
>
> We discuss key topics such as the roles of Scrum Master and Product Owner, navigating technical complexity, and leadership through purpose-driven goal-setting. Practical frameworks like the Eisenhower Matrix improve prioritization, while insights into senior problem-solving and motivation reveal how individual decisions impact system-wide alignment.
>
> Additional themes include AI’s role in cognition, servant leadership as a foundation for alignment, and how metrics shape behavior toward shared purposes. Whether you’re a software engineer or strategist, this blog helps bridge isolated parts to see how systems harmonize—fulfilling purpose with coherence and impact.

## Working with the Eisenhower Matrix

There are two parts that are hard in this system for present information. One is prioritization. The other is acting according to the system.

Whether a task is important or urgent is not obvious very often. But there are a set of questions that can help to classify:

* Will it have an impact on my life/job/goals/company in a year if the task is not done? If yes, then the task might be important
* Is there a due date for a task within the next couple of days? If yes, then the task might be urgent

Acting based on the Eisenhower Matrix is the other hard part. Having a good list of tasks does not help if you are not working according to that list.

## Evolution and Innovation in Software Engineering

Write about why having contact with the **customer** / **environment** is indispensable for a software engineering team to be able to come up with novel and innovative solutions

A 2017 paper by Alyssa Adams, Hector Zenil, Paul C. W. Davies, and Sara I. Walker titled [_Formal Definitions of Unbounded Evolution and Innovation Reveal Universal Mechanisms for Open-Ended Evolution in Dynamical Systems_](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-00810-8) explains how this works:

* The researchers recognized that a system capable of innovation must necessarily consist of two interconnected subsystems: the _organism_ and the _environment_. The two systems are intricately coupled, meaning that changes in one impact the other and vice versa.
* In their study, Adams et al. introduced a modified CA model where not only the states of the cells evolved but also the rules that governed their behaviour. By observing the dynamics of this novel model, the researchers discovered that the interplay between the organism and the environment played a crucial role in driving innovation.
* Furthermore, the study found that having feedback loops between the organism and the environment _amplifies_ the system’s potential for innovation. These feedback loops create a constant cycle of adaptation and selection, enabling the system to generate novel forms and behaviours continually.
* Besides 'adaption' also 'exadaption' is a mechanism though which complex systems can innovate - a trait of a system that can be used to address new requirements the environment enforces. Medicaments are sometimes found to be useful for other treatments than for what they have initially been designed. Feathers as a requirement for birds to fly have most likely not evolved to support flying but for other purposes (insulation or display, ...)

## The task of a leader is to become unnecessary

There are different kinds of leaders. Those that can lead with charisma, those that lead by power and many flavors more. The ones I admire the most are of a different kind. It is those that continuously try to become unnecessary. Not in a way such that they don't take over their responsibilities or that they shy from work. But in a way that they structure the system so it can perform without them being 'on the stage' all the time. If the leader has to be present, he has done a poor job or has not yet finished his job at least. If he has to present then the system contains friction, accountability and authrority are not aligned and the like. Good leaders are impediment removers and designers of system.
