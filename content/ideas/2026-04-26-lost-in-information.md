There is a specific feeling of being lost in information: a hundred tabs open, a thousand articles saved, a feed that never ends — and the dawning certainty that none of it is turning into anything. The feeling is not a failure of discipline. It is the natural result of treating the wrong resource as the scarce one.

<!--more-->

## The Scarcity Is Not Information

We have been taught to hoard information as if it were scarce. It is not. It is the cheapest abundant resource in human history. The genuinely scarce resource is *attention* — the bounded, perishable capacity to turn information into understanding.

The moment the scarcity moves from information to attention, the whole strategy changes:

- **Collecting is replaced by curating.** Saving everything is the cheapest possible activity and the most worthless. Curating is deciding what is worth your attention before you spend it.
- **Abundance becomes a design problem.** An infinite feed is not a resource; it is a flood. The system must filter, and the filter is the design.
- **Depth is bought with neglect.** Attention spent on one thing is attention not spent on everything else. Every "keep up" is a "fall behind" somewhere — the ledger is unavoidable.

## The Navigation Skills

Being lost in information is a navigation problem, and navigation has specific skills:

1. **A destination.** If you cannot say what you are looking for, the flood will decide for you. An intention is the only compass that works.
2. **A filter that runs before you, not after.** Question your sources at the intake level, not while reading. The cost of consuming is paid twice: in time and in attention — and both should be guarded before they are spent.
3. **A way to stop.** The skill of closing the tab, unsubscribing, and leaving the feed unread is the most underrated. The information will still be there; your attention will not.
4. **A place to put what matters.** The Zettelkasten discipline applies here: capture *thoughts* — statements that connect — not links. A saved link without a captured thought is attention spent on logistics.

{{< alert >}}
**The two-question test.**

Before consuming anything, ask: *"Will I act on this?"* and *"Will this still matter in a month?"* If the answer to both is no, you are collecting, not learning — and the collection is costing you the only resource you cannot replace.
{{< /alert >}}

## The End of the Feed

The feed is designed to be endless, and "catching up" is a game you cannot win — there is no end state. The alternative is not to read less. It is to read *for a purpose*, so that the information becomes material for thinking instead of a flood that washes over it. Lost in information is a choice of posture: collector or navigator. Only one of them ever gets anywhere.
