There is a particular kind of person who is always right and never gets anything done. They know the better architecture, the better process, the better strategy — and none of it matters, because knowledge that does not change what happens is indistinguishable from noise.

<!--more-->

## Rightness Is Not a Strategy

Software teams are full of correct opinions that are never executed: the refactor that would fix everything, the tool that would replace three broken ones, the process that would end the chaos. They are correct. And the system remains exactly as it was.

Effectiveness is not about having the right answer. It is about converting an answer into a change in the world. The engineer who ships a mediocre solution that works has done more than the engineer who has maintained a perfect design document for two years.

## Execution Is a Discipline

Being effective is a skill, not a temperament. It has concrete practices:

- **Choose, then commit.** A decision made and followed is worth more than the perfect decision made too late — or never.
- **Shrink the loop.** The faster you close the gap between deciding and doing, the more you learn, and the less momentum you lose.
- **Do the boring parts.** Execution is mostly unglamorous: the follow-up, the merge, the deploy, the email that unblocks someone.
- **Measure by shipped change.** Ask what changed because you were involved this month. If the honest answer is "nothing," the opinions were decoration.

{{< alert >}}
**The uncomfortable question.**

At the end of every project, ask not "was I right?" but "did anything change because of me?" Effectiveness is the only metric that pays.
{{< /alert >}}

## Why Being Right Feels Like Enough

The trap is seductive because being right *feels* like work. The design critique, the well-argued objection, the correct forecast — they produce the same satisfaction as accomplishment without any of the risk. That is precisely why they are so common, and why the people who consistently execute stand out so sharply.

The most useful mindset shift: treat an unexecuted correct opinion as a liability. You now own it, and the longer it sits unacted, the less you are worth on that topic.
