Our body is a relentless energy saver. Stop walking much, and the leg muscles shrink to the size that is actually needed. Stop using your range of motion, and the body adapts — you get stiff. The brain is one of the largest energy consumers in the body, so the body's saving instinct applies to it with extra force. What, then, happens when we more and more rely on AIs to do the thinking for us?

<!--more-->

## Use It or Lose It Is a Law, Not a Slogan

The principle is biological, not motivational. Tissue that is not loaded gets reduced because maintaining it costs energy. The brain follows the same economics: pathways that are not exercised are pruned, skills that are not used decay. This is not a metaphor — it is the operating mode of a system that evolved to spend as little as possible.

The question that follows is uncomfortable: if thinking is a muscle, what happens when we stop loading it?

## The New Distribution of Thinking

Before AI, thinking was necessarily in the loop: to get an answer you had to think it, because nothing else would. AI changes the distribution of effort. The writing can be generated, the code produced, the analysis drafted. The thinking that *could* be skipped now is skipped — and each skip is a rep that does not happen.

The relevant concept is what the waitbutwhy article on *The Cook and the Chef* makes vivid: the difference between knowing how to operate a process and knowing how it works. If we never cook, we may keep the ability to eat. The question is whether we keep the ability to cook — or whether the recipes quietly become the only thing we know.

## What Might Atrophy

If the body's principles apply to the mind, the candidates for decline are precisely the skills that AI now exercises for us:

- **Sustained attention and deep reading** — replaced by retrieval and skimming.
- **Constructing an argument from first principles** — replaced by accepting generated conclusions.
- **Recalling and connecting knowledge** — replaced by search.
- **Tolerating the discomfort of thinking** — the hardest one. Thinking is effortful; if the effort is always skippable, the tolerance for it fades too.

## The Countermeasure

None of this is an argument against the tools. It is an argument for deliberate loading — the same way an athlete trains the muscles that work has stopped training:

1. **Do hard thinking on a schedule.** Write without a generator, solve problems without a search, reconstruct arguments without a source. The load is the point.
2. **Use AI to extend, not replace, the rep.** Draft with the tool, then rewrite by hand from understanding. The chef still makes the dish, even if they taste the machine's version first.
3. **Keep the uncomfortable gap.** The pause where you *don't* know the answer yet is the exercise. Fill it instantly, and you have outsourced the whole rep.

The body gives back exactly what you load it with — and takes back exactly what you stop loading. The brain will not be an exception just because the machines got good.
