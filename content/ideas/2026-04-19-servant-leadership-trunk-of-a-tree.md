The trunk of a tree produces nothing you can eat. No fruit, no leaves, no seed. Its entire function is to carry water and nutrients from the roots to the branches that produce the visible results. A tree does not grow because the trunk grows. It grows because the trunk serves the parts that produce — and yet without the trunk, none of it happens.

<!--more-->

## The Trunk Does Not Take Credit

Nobody praises the trunk at harvest time. The fruit is the visible success, the leaves catch the light, and the trunk stands quietly in the middle, doing the unglamorous work of distribution. Servant leadership is exactly this: the leader is the trunk that enables the visible producers.

The instinct to be the fruit is powerful. The organization celebrates the visible: the shipped product, the closed deal, the feature that wins. The trunk's work — removing the obstacle, securing the resource, clearing the path, distributing the support — is invisible. A servant leader has to be comfortable with an entire career of invisible work whose success is measured by *other* people's results.

## What the Trunk Actually Does

Look at what the trunk provides, and you have the servant leader's job description:

- **Transport — the connection between strategy and execution.** The trunk carries direction down and signals up. Without it, the roots (resources, intent) never reach the branches (the work).
- **Structure — the shape that lets branches reach for light.** The leader provides the guardrails and stability that let others grow in a useful direction instead of randomly.
- **Repair — the healing of damage.** When a branch breaks, the trunk keeps the tree alive. When a team fails, the servant leader absorbs the impact and redirects energy into recovery.

None of these produce visible fruit. All of them determine whether fruit is produced at all.

{{< alert >}}
**The harvest test.**

At the end of the season, whose results are visible? If they are always yours, you are a branch pretending to be a trunk. The trunk's harvest is measured entirely in what the branches produced — not in itself.
{{< /alert >}}

## Service First, or Not at All

Robert Greenleaf's formulation is exact: the servant leader serves *first* — the service is the motivation, not the path to power. The moment the trunk starts demanding the fruit, it stops being a trunk. A leader who leads to be served produces a tree with a very short trunk and a lot of broken branches.

The practice, then, is a daily reorientation:

1. **Ask what the branches need from you today** — not what you need from them.
2. **Take the invisible work first** — the unblocking, the protecting, the resourcing.
3. **Measure your day by their progress, not your output.**

The trunk is the least glamorous part of the tree. It is also the only reason the tree exists.
