A company is a small planet, and like every planet it exists because two forces balance. **Gravity** pulls matter toward the center — the core business, the shared systems, the standards, the culture. **Centrifugal force** — *Fliehkraft* — flings matter outward, away from the center — the experiments, the new products, the autonomy of teams. Too much of one, and the planet either collapses or flies apart.

<!--more-->

## The Two Forces

**Gravity** is what keeps a company coherent. The shared platform, the common processes, the single brand, the one way of doing things. Gravity is what lets a thousand employees act like one organization instead of a thousand solo operators. It is also what holds everything in place — including, if it gets too strong, everything that should be allowed to leave.

**Centrifugal force** is what pushes new things outward. It is the innovation that does not fit the core, the team that wants to try a different process, the product that starts as a sideline. Centrifugal force is how a company explores — and it is also how a company falls apart, if its parts simply spin away with nothing holding them.

## The Balance Is the System

Watch a company and you will see it constantly oscillating between the two extremes:

- **Gravity wins:** standardization everywhere, one platform, one process, one way to think. Predictable, efficient, and slowly incapable of anything new. Every new idea is absorbed, normalized, and dies of sameness.
- **Centrifugal force wins:** every team spins off its own stack, its own processes, its own product. Innovative, lively — and incoherent. The planet's surface is moving in ten different directions, and the core can no longer hold.

The healthy company is not the one that eliminates one force. It is the one that keeps both in tension — a planet with enough gravity to stay whole and enough spin to fling off new moons.

{{< alert >}}
**The gravity test.**

Ask where a genuinely new idea goes in your company. If the answer is "into the standard process," gravity has won. If the answer is "everywhere, in every direction," the centrifugal force has won. The interesting answer is: *"somewhere the core still holds it."*
{{< /alert >}}

## Designing the Balance

Balance is not a state; it is a set of deliberate structures:

1. **A core with real gravity.** The shared platform and standards are the center — but only the things that must be shared should be at the center. Gravity concentrated on the right few things is strength; spread everywhere it is a black hole.
2. **Allowed orbits.** Innovation needs a defined distance from the core: new ideas can spin out, run with different rules, prove themselves — and then be pulled back in, or launched as their own body. Orbit, not escape.
3. **Visible tension.** The leadership conversation should be about the forces, out loud: *where do we hold together, and where do we let go?* A company that never argues about this has already picked a side by default.

The planet metaphor has one more lesson: planets do not choose their balance once. They adjust it continuously, against the pull of everything around them. So do companies.
