Every annual report says it: *"Our people are our greatest asset."* Then the same company opens a spreadsheet of *resources* and optimizes their utilization like they optimize a server fleet. These are not two words for the same thing. They describe opposite relationships, and the contradiction quietly shapes everything.

<!--more-->

## What an Asset Is

An asset is something you value, maintain, and invest in. It appreciates over time if you care for it. You protect it, you repair it, you plan its future. Nobody writes off an asset casually.

## What a Resource Is

A resource is something you consume. It has utilization, capacity, and cost. You allocate it, you swap it out, you optimize it. A resource is *interchangeable* by definition — that is what makes it useful in a plan.

The difference is not semantic. Utilization thinking and appreciation thinking produce completely different decisions.

## The Contradiction in Practice

Watch how the words get used and the priorities become visible:

- **Planning**: "resource planning" is about fitting people into slots. "Asset development" is about growing people into bigger slots.
- **Allocation**: resources get moved to where the demand is highest. Assets get kept where they create the most long-term value — even when short-term demand says otherwise.
- **Failure**: a resource that underperforms is replaced. An asset that underperforms is invested in — or honestly retired.
- **Utilization**: the goal for a resource is 100% full. The goal for an asset is long-term yield. A fully utilized asset has no room left for investment.

The tragedy is not that companies use one word or the other. It is that they claim the asset word while operating with the resource model. People feel it instantly: treated as fungible, optimized for throughput, swapped between projects. The annual report says "asset"; the resource plan says "resource" — and people read the plan.

{{< alert >}}
**The test.**

If a team member receives a better offer, does your company treat it like losing a depreciating asset or like breaking a tool? The answer tells you which model you actually run — regardless of what the value statement says.
{{< /alert >}}

## What the Asset Model Requires

Treating people as assets is expensive and uncomfortable:

- It means *capacity slack* — deliberately keeping people below maximum utilization so they can learn and improve.
- It means *long horizons* — investing in someone who will only pay back over years.
- It means *honest failure* — accepting that not every hire works out, and handling that with respect, not as scrap.

That is a much harder management job than resource allocation. Which is precisely why so many companies talk the asset talk and run the resource model.
